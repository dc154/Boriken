Mofongo

Mofongo is a signature Puerto Rican dish made from twice-fried green plantains mashed in a wooden mortar (pilón) with garlic and crispy pork cracklings (chicharrón). The dense, savory mash is often formed into a ball and served stuffed with seafood or meat.

  • Prep time: 20 minutes
  • Cook time: 25 minutes
  • Serving: 4 (as a hearty side dish)
  • Tags: Puerto Rican, Side Dish, Plantain, Garlic

Ingredients

  • 4 large green plantains (unripe)
  • 3 cups vegetable or canola oil (for frying)
  • 1 tsp salt, divided
  • 1/4 cup pork cracklings (chicharrón) or bacon bits
  • 4-6 cloves fresh garlic, minced
  • 1/4 cup warm chicken broth (or water)
  • 1 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper to taste

Instructions

Part 1: Prepare and Fry the Plantains

  1. Peel the Plantains: Slice off both ends of the plantains. Score the peel lengthwise in three or four places, cutting shallowly only through the skin. Pry the peel off using the tip of a knife.
  2. Soak: Cut the peeled plantains into 3/4 inch thick rounds. Place the rounds in a bowl of cold water with 1/2 teaspoon of salt for about 10 minutes. Drain and pat them completely dry.
  3. First Fry: Heat the oil in a heavy-bottomed pan to 300F degrees. Fry the plantain rounds in batches for about 5 to 7 minutes until they turn light yellow and are soft but not crispy. They should not be golden brown. Remove and drain on paper towels.
  4. Smash and Second Fry: While still warm, use a flat object (like the bottom of a glass) or a wooden mallet to smash each plantain round flat—about 1/4 to 1/2 inch thick. Return the smashed pieces to the hot oil and fry again at 350F (175C) or 2 to 3 minutes until golden brown and crispy. Drain and sprinkle with the remaining.

    5. Part 2: Mash and Form the Mofongo

    1. Prepare Garlic Mix: In a separate small bowl, combine the minced garlic, extra virgin olive oil, and a pinch of salt and pepper to create a quick paste.
    2. Mash the Ingredients: Place the twice-fried plantain pieces, pork cracklings, and the garlic paste into a large pilón (mortar and pestle).
    3. Moisten: Begin mashing the mixture together. As it mashes, slowly add the warm chicken broth, a tablespoon at a time, until the mixture is cohesive, moist, and slightly sticky. Do not over-mash; you want a lumpy texture, not a smooth paste.
    4. Serve: Form the mash into four small, dense balls. Serve immediately as a side dish, or press the mash into a bowl and invert it onto a plate to serve as a base for stewed meat, shrimp, or a traditional broth (caldo).

Caldo Recipe

    Ingredients

  • Chicken Broth - 4 cups (low sodium is best)
  • Sofrito - 2 tbsp (or use 1 tbsp finely minced onion and 1 tbsp finely minced bell pepper)
  • Garlic - 2 cloves, minced
  • Sazón (optional) - 1 packet with Annatto/Achiote (for color)
  • Tomato Sauce - 1 tbsp
  • Cilantro - 1/4 cup, fresh, chopped
  • Adobo or Salt & Pepper - To taste
  • Olive Oil - 1 tsp

  • Sauté Aromatics

    1. In a medium saucepan, heat the olive oil over medium heat. Add the sofrito (or onion and pepper mix) and the minced garlic. Sauté for 3 to 4 minutes until fragrant.

  • Build Flavor Base

    2. Stir in the tomato sauce and the optional packet of Sazón. Cook for 1 minute, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom of the pan.

  • Simmer the Broth

    3. Pour in the chicken broth. Bring the mixture to a simmer over medium-high heat.

  • Season and Finish

    4. Once simmering, reduce the heat to low. Add the Adobo (or salt and pepper) to taste. Stir in the fresh cilantro.

  • Serve:

    5. Let the broth gently simmer for 10 to 15 minutes to allow the flavors to deepen. Serve the hot caldo in small bowls alongside the Mofongo for dipping, or pour it directly over the plantain mash before serving.
